# DRC Conflict Zones and Agricultural Areas Research

## Conflict Zones in DRC

### Major Conflict Areas
- **North Kivu Province**: Accounts for the largest share of violent events, driven by political rivalries, disputes over land, mineral interests, and foreign intervention.
- **South Kivu Province**: Significant conflict area with recent M23 rebel activity.
- **Ituri Province**: One of the most affected areas by violent clashes.

### Key Conflict Actors
- **M23 (March 23 Movement)**: Formed in 2012 by former Congolese soldiers, mainly from the Tutsi ethnic group. Recently captured Goma (January 2025), Bukavu (February 2025), and Walikale (March 2025).
- **Allied Democratic Forces (ADF)**: Armed group with reported ties to the Islamic State, responsible for over 1,000 reported fatalities in 2023.
- **Wazalendo**: Local self-defense groups incorporating existing armed groups.
- **FDLR (Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda)**: Group with links to the former Hutu regime in Rwanda.

### Humanitarian Impact
- More than 7 million people are displaced, including 3.8 million in North and South Kivu provinces.
- Nearly 780,000 people were forced to flee their homes between November 2024 and January 2025 alone.
- Since January 2025, more than 100,000 refugees have crossed into neighboring countries.
- 25.6 million people (nearly a quarter of the population) face acute food insecurity.
- 2.7 million people in North Kivu, South Kivu, and Ituri were already facing severe food shortage before the latest escalation.

## Agricultural Areas and Potential

### Agricultural Land
- 80 million hectares of arable land, of which less than 10% is currently exploited.
- 4 million hectares of irrigated land potential.
- Grasslands and savannahs can support a livestock population of about 40 million head of cattle.
- Tropical forests occupy 135 million hectares (52% of territory).
- Fisheries potential estimated at 707,000 tons of fish per year.

### Major Crops and Production (2018 data)
1. **Cassava**: 29.9 million tons (3rd largest producer in the world)
2. **Plantains**: 4.7 million tons (largest producer in the world)
3. **Maize**: 2 million tons
4. **Palm Oil**: 1.1 million tons
5. **Rice**: 990 thousand tons
6. **Sweet Potato**: 384 thousand tons
7. **Banana**: 309 thousand tons
8. **Peanuts**: 307 thousand tons
9. **Mango/Mangosteen/Guava**: 213 thousand tons

### Cash Crops
- Coffee (main agricultural export)
- Rubber
- Palm oil
- Cocoa
- Tea
- Cotton
- Sugar

### Agricultural Challenges
- Poor internal transportation system impeding effective national urban food-supply system
- Conflict disrupting agricultural activities
- Limited commercial agricultural production (most producers engaged in subsistence farming)
- Food production insufficient to meet the country's needs
- Looting of humanitarian infrastructure affecting food distribution

### Climate and Geography
- Diverse climates supported by an important hydrographic network
- Not drought-prone
- Variety of agricultural zones from rainforest to savanna

## Implications for AgriSight Project

1. **Priority Monitoring Areas**: North Kivu, South Kivu, and Ituri provinces should be prioritized for satellite monitoring due to the severity of conflict and food insecurity.

2. **Key Crops to Monitor**: 
   - Cassava, plantains, and maize as the primary food security crops
   - Coffee, palm oil, and cocoa as important cash crops for economic stability

3. **Challenges to Address**:
   - Monitoring agricultural activities in conflict zones with limited ground access
   - Tracking displacement impacts on agricultural production
   - Identifying areas of agricultural stress due to conflict disruption
   - Monitoring land use changes as populations flee conflict areas

4. **Opportunities**:
   - Vast untapped agricultural potential (90% of arable land unexploited)
   - Potential to support government initiatives for agro-industrial parks
   - Providing data-driven insights for humanitarian interventions in food-insecure areas
   - Supporting resilience-building in agricultural communities affected by conflict
